The Science page (`/science`) was redesigned as a calm, editorial "Science & Efficacy" page for new mothers. It uses a new warm palette: a cream (#FBF9F5) and oat (#EFECE6) background, muted sage (#8A9A86) for dividers, icon frames and the diagram, deep slate teal (#3B5249) and espresso (#2C2A29) for text, and dusty rose (#D8A48F) for the main call to action. The rose button uses dark text so it stays readable. The colors were added as named theme tokens, so the rest of the storefront is unchanged.

The page has six sections:

- **Hero:** "The Clinical Science Behind SmoothScar," with an empathetic introduction.
- **The Anatomy of Healing:** four steps, from the incision closing and collagen remodeling to how the BioRNA-C micro-tips engage the skin. It ends with a simple diagram of the patch on the skin.
- **Quality & Safety:** six trust factors with sage line icons, each taken from the BioRNA-C packaging or the manufacturer: hypoallergenic, suitable for sensitive skin, hydrates and protects, needle-free micro-tips, developed at a medical-school incubator, and patents filed in the USA, Singapore and China.
- **Clinical Research:** large-type callouts describing the published randomized, single-blinded, intra-individual controlled trial.
- **Your Care Team:** suggested questions for an OB-GYN, a dermatologist and a pelvic floor physical therapist.
- **Closing CTA:** a rose "Explore BioRNA-C" button and a link to the brochure.

Some of the example content in the request was left out on purpose because it is inaccurate or unsubstantiated for this product. BioRNA is a hyaluronic-acid micro-tip patch, not medical-grade silicone. Its FDA-cleared and BPA-free status hasn't been verified. The "94% of mothers" figure has no source. No real clinician endorsements exist yet. Claims that the patch accelerates healing or flattens collagen were also avoided. The page has two empty, editable lists, one for approved efficacy metrics and one for expert quotes. When you add entries after a compliance review and written permission from the people quoted, they replace the study-design callouts and the care-team questions on the page.
